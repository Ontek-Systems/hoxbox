## Page 4 — Pre-Construction Service

**OUR PRE CONSTRUCTION SERVICE**

> Pre-construction services are the preliminary planning, budgeting, and design analysis
> phases conducted by a contractor before formal construction begins. These services
> typically include feasibility studies, value engineering, budget estimations, scheduling,
> constructability reviews, and permitting. They are usually formalised via a
> Pre-Construction Services Agreement (PCSA) to reduce project risk.

**KEY COMPONENTS OF PRE-CONSTRUCTION SERVICES**

- **Cost Estimation & Budgeting:** Establishing accurate budget estimates, cost plans, and Bills of Quantities to ensure the project is financially viable.
- **Value Engineering:** Analyzing design choices to maximize value and minimize costs without sacrificing quality.
- **Constructability Reviews:** Evaluating plans to ensure they can be efficiently built, which minimizes errors and disputes.
- **Scheduling & Planning:** Developing a master schedule, identifying long-lead items, and planning logistics.
- **Risk Assessment & Mitigation:** Identifying potential risks (e.g., site conditions, legal issues) early.
- **Permitting & Approvals:** Handling necessary approvals and collaborating with local authorities on compliance.
- **Site Investigations:** Assessing site conditions before mobilization.

Note: this page uses US spelling ("maximize", "minimize", "mobilization") inconsistently with the rest of the document, which is otherwise UK spelling ("recognise", "colour"-style). Preserve as-is; flag if copy needs to be normalised to UK English sitewide.

## Page 6 — Case Study: British Museum

**BRITISH MUSEUM**
London WC1 / 50,000 sq ft / 9 weeks
Client: CBRE, on behalf of The British Museum

**Project Overview**
> Appointed by CBRE to deliver a highly sensitive refurbishment at 89 Great Russell
> Street, this project involved the full replacement of the roof structure on a Grade I
> listed building forming part of The British Museum estate. The work demanded
> meticulous planning and execution within a live heritage environment of national
> significance.

**Scope and Delivery**
> The project encompassed the renewal of the existing roof fabric, including the
> installation of a handcrafted Welsh slate roof and the construction of traditional
> lead-clad dormers. All interventions were carried out in strict accordance with historic
> building conservation standards, ensuring that materials, detailing, and workmanship
> reflected the original 19th-century character while meeting contemporary performance
> requirements.
>
> Working within the Museum's operational constraints, the team coordinated carefully
> with heritage consultants and conservation officers to balance preservation with modern
> technical demands.
>
> The project was delivered to programme within nine weeks, with minimal disruption to
> the surrounding Museum environment.

**Facts:** Grade I listed building, 89 Great Russell Street, handcrafted Welsh slate roof, traditional lead-clad dormers, 19th-century character, delivered to programme in 9 weeks.

## Known inconsistencies in the source PDF

Flag these with the client before republishing as fact — likely artefacts of reusing a slide template rather than deliberate:

1. **British Museum — 50,000 sq ft.** A roof replacement at a single Georgian townhouse (89 Great Russell Street) described as 50,000 sq ft seems large; the same figure (50,000 sq ft) also appears on three unrelated case studies (New City College, Sodexo Ipswich, Sodexo Colchester), suggesting it may be a placeholder value rather than a verified figure for each project.
2. **The Lightbowne Ski Chalet — duration.** Header says "18 months"; body copy says "Completed over 2 years." These conflict.
3. **The Lightbowne Ski Chalet vs. Craftsbury — identical dimensions.** Both list "430 SQ M / 4,628 SQ FT / 18 months" despite being unrelated projects (a Méribel ski chalet vs. an Essex barn conversion). Almost certainly a copy/paste error in the deck.
4. **Esher Office Delapidation vs. Production Factory Offices.** Near-identical scope wording ("full strip-out back to the core structure") and both are dilapidation/reinstatement projects — worth double-checking these are in fact two distinct projects, not a duplicated entry.
5. **Spelling of "dilapidation".** Page 7's heading reads "DELAPIDATION" (non-standard); body copy on the same page correctly spells it "dilapidation." Standardise to "dilapidation" going forward.
6. **US vs UK spelling.** Page 4 (Pre-Construction Service) uses US spellings ("maximize," "minimize," "mobilization") while the rest of the document uses UK English. Normalise to UK spelling if reusing this section's copy.
7. **"Vinal wrap"** (page 3 service list) is a typo for "Vinyl wrap."
